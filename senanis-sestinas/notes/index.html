Senanis Sestinas Notes

How a Senanis Sestina moves

A traditional sestina is built from six recurring end-words. They first appear in six lines, then return in a different order through five more six-line stanzas. A final three-line envoi brings all six words together.

A Senanis Sestina joins that pattern to the central constraint of Senanis: every line is also a six-word Senanis Moment. Each line can offer a small image or reflection of its own, while its returning end-word connects it to lines elsewhere in the poem.

Repetition does not make the words stand still. As the surrounding language changes, a word may shift in tone, part of speech, image, or implication. The form invites attention to both recurrence and difference.

In the envoi, all six returning words appear across three final six-word lines, two words to a line. Their exact positions within those lines are not fixed; the language may determine where each word falls.

The pattern provides the structure, but not a single interpretation. As with other Senanis forms, the aim is to notice what constraint makes possible and where the returning words take you.
